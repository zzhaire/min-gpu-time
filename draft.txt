
        Deep learning (DL) cluster scheduling research has long focused primarily on minimizing job completion time (JCT) or ensuring fairness, typically treating hardware resources as exclusive commodities to avoid performance jitter. However, this risk-averse strategy has caused severe \textbf{Resource Bloat} in homogeneous clusters---to maintain exclusive performance, expensive GPU memory and computing power are largely wasted due to fragmentation. To address this, we propose \textbf{Eco-Pollux}, a scheduling framework aimed at cost optimization, which seeks to gain higher resource density by allowing controlled interference. Unlike traditional schedulers that prioritize speedup, Eco-Pollux introduces an \textbf{Interference-Aware Unit Pricing (IAUP) model}, which mathematically incentivizes the scheduler to pack compatible tasks onto shared GPUs, thereby translating physical interference into reduced economic costs. To address the risk of topology fragmentation caused by sharing, we further design a \textbf{Topology-Aware Patience mechanism} that avoids high cross-rack communication penalties by employing strategic short-term waiting. Large-scale trace-driven simulations demonstrate that, in high-load scenarios, Eco-Pollux reduces Total GPU Hours by \textbf{up to 12.3\%}, significantly outperforming existing adaptive schedulers (such as Pollux) without compromising the fairness of long-tail jobs. These results confirm that sacrificing marginal individual speed in exchange for higher packing density is an effective paradigm for minimizing the operational costs of AI infrastructure.
